## 2. Shipping and phyto-cert reality for China to Israel

- Method: China plant exporters almost always ship live plants and TC plantlets
  by air, using express couriers (DHL, EMS) or air cargo. Typical air transit
  plus inspection runs roughly 5 to 10 days; on cheaper or congested routes it
  can stretch toward two weeks, which is the danger zone for live material.
- Phyto certificate: in China the phytosanitary certificate is issued by the
  government quarantine authority, and only registered exporters working with
  compliant nurseries can obtain one. A legitimate exporter should include the
  phyto cert (plus commercial invoice, packing list, and air waybill) with every
  shipment. Since the Israeli import license requires a phyto cert per shipment,
  make "phyto cert issued for Israel, every shipment" a hard qualifying question.
- Phyto cert timing: the certificate is generally issued close to shipment and
  has a short validity window (commonly 7 to 14 days), so it must be matched to
  the actual dispatch date, not issued weeks early.
- DDP / door-to-door: some exporters offer DDP (Delivered Duty Paid) or
  door-to-door, but this was NOT confirmed for any specific candidate above and
  was not clearly advertised for Israel. Ask explicitly. Note that even under
  DDP, Israeli plant-quarantine clearance still depends on the importer's license
  and a valid phyto cert, so DDP mainly removes freight/customs hassle, it does
  not remove the need for your own license and correct paperwork.
- TC advantage: sterile in-vitro (in-agar) plantlets are generally easier to
  clear and survive transit better than mature potted plants, because they are
  pathogen-tested and sealed. Deflasked (ex-agar) material is more fragile in a
  1 to 2 week transit and needs better packing.

## 3. Price positioning vs Thailand (rough, treat as order-of-magnitude)

The web did not return a clean apples-to-apples China vs Thailand wholesale
price sheet, so this is directional, not exact.

- China's structural edge: lower labor cost, large government-supported TC lab
  capacity, and very high volume output. For common and mid-tier TC aroids bought
  in quantity, China is generally positioned as the cheaper, higher-volume
  option, which is exactly the "competitive-priced volume" role being sought.
- Rough plantlet price bands seen for TC aroids (indicative, per plant, before
  freight and before your margin):
  - Basic / non-variegated TC plantlets (e.g. Monstera adansonii): about
    15 to 30 USD range at the retail-ish end; true wholesale in quantity should
    come in lower.
  - Pre-acclimated rarer forms (e.g. Thai Constellation): roughly 40 to 90 USD.
  - Fully acclimated variegated cultivars (Albo, Aurea, etc.): about
    100 to 250+ USD.
- Where Thailand still wins: ultra-rare, specific named clones and high-end
  variegates. Thailand's rare-aroid ecosystem is deep, so for the top-tier "grail"
  items Thailand is often the better and sometimes the only realistic source.
- Practical read: use China for volume on the three core genera (Alocasia,
  Monstera, Philodendron) at competitive cost, and keep Thailand for ultra-rare.
  Do not assume China is cheaper on a specific rare clone until you have a real
  quote; freight and MOQ can erase the unit-price advantage.

Add freight to every comparison: air freight commonly adds on the order of
1.50 to 3.00 USD per kg, which matters a lot on small orders.

## 4. Red flags / scam-avoidance checklist

General China B2B (Alibaba / Made-in-China):
- "Gold Supplier" or verified badges are paid memberships, not a guarantee of
  honesty. Verify independently.
- Big pressure to leave Trade Assurance or platform escrow and pay by direct
  bank wire or crypto to a personal account. That removes your safety net.
- Brand-new store with suspiciously perfect reviews and huge claimed transaction
  volume can be fabricated.
- Refusal to sell a small paid sample, or demanding 100% upfront on a first
  order, is a classic warning sign.
- Price that is far below everyone else. On rare plants, "too cheap" usually
  means mislabeled, wrong cultivar, or a mass reversion (a green plant sold as
  variegated).

Live-plant / rare-aroid specific:
- Stock-photo catalogs. Insist on fresh photos or short video of the ACTUAL
  current stock, ideally with a dated note or their own labeling in frame.
- Variegation stability: cheap TC "variegated" aroids frequently revert to plain
  green after deflasking. Ask about genetic/variegation stability and whether the
  variegation is stable in their line.
- Packing for long transit: for a 1 to 2 week trip, ask exactly how they pack
  (sealed in-agar flasks vs bare-root, moisture control, heat packs in cold
  months, insulation, box ventilation). Vague answers predict dead-on-arrival.
- Dead-on-arrival policy: get their DOA / replacement terms in writing BEFORE
  paying. Live goods on slow shipping are the top loss risk.
- MOQ traps: a low advertised unit price can hide a high minimum order quantity
  or a "mixed species not allowed" rule that forces you to over-buy. Confirm MOQ
  and whether mixed-genus orders are allowed.
- Paperwork bait-and-switch: confirm the phyto certificate is actually issued for
  Israel every shipment and is not quietly dropped to shave cost. No phyto cert
  means your license cannot clear the box.

CITES note: the common houseplant aroid genera here (Alocasia, Monstera,
Philodendron) are, as a rule, NOT CITES-listed, so a CITES permit is normally not
the issue for standard variegated cultivars. CITES only bites if a specific
species is listed. The real gating documents for Israel are your import license
plus a valid phytosanitary certificate per shipment. If a supplier claims a
genus "needs CITES" for ordinary Monstera/Philodendron, treat that as a knowledge
gap on their side and double-check.

## 5. Ready-to-send first-contact qualifying message

Short, professional English. Paste to any candidate and swap the bracketed bits.
It is built to disqualify time-wasters fast.

---

Subject: Wholesale TC aroids for import to Israel, a few qualifying questions

Hello,

I am a licensed plant importer in Israel (Ramat Gan) and I am looking for a
regular China supplier of tissue-culture and variegated aroids: Alocasia,
Monstera, and Philodendron. Before we go further, could you confirm the
following:

1. Do you ship live plants / TC plantlets to Israel, and have you done so before?
2. Do you issue an official phytosanitary certificate for Israel with every
   shipment (I hold a valid Israeli import license and this is required)?
3. Do you offer DDP or door-to-door, or is it EXW / freight-on-me? Which courier
   (DHL, EMS, air cargo) and typical transit time to Israel?
4. What is your MOQ, and can I mix genera (Alocasia + Monstera + Philodendron) in
   one order? Do you sell a paid sample order first?
5. Which of the three genera do you actually have in stock right now, and can you
   send fresh photos or a short video of the current stock (not catalog images)?
6. How do you pack TC / live plants for a 1 to 2 week transit, and what is your
   dead-on-arrival replacement policy?
7. Do you supply in-agar (sealed flask) or deflasked plantlets, and is the
   variegation stable in your lines?

A short price list for the items you have in stock, with per-plant prices at your
MOQ, would help me move quickly.

Thank you,
[Name]
[Business name]
Ramat Gan, Israel [postal code 5229581 if a shipping quote needs it]
