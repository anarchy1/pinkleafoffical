The standard Pink Princess puts its pink down in one large block. The Marble

### English

```
Philodendron Angela Aurea 🌿

Most people treat variegation as one thing. It is not.

White tissue has nothing in it. No chlorophyll, no other pigment, which is
why it scorches easily and dries at the edges.

Gold tissue has carotenoids, the same pigments that make a carrot orange.
They produce no energy, but they do absorb some of the light and shield the
tissue underneath. That is why an aurea plant is tougher than an albo one and
far less likely to burn.

The second thing worth knowing: an Angela leaf changes shape as it ages.
Young leaves are relatively simple, and as the plant climbs the lobes
lengthen and the splitting becomes dramatic. A pole is not decoration, it is
what lets the plant reach its adult form.

Swipe for the full breakdown 👉

Message us for availability 💬
```

### Why this angle

Every seller posts a photograph of gold variegation and calls it stunning.
None of them explain why gold is hardier than white, and that is the question
behind the one the customer actually asks, which is whether they will kill it.
The shape-changes-with-age point does the same job for the price: it tells a
buyer that the plant they receive is not the finished article, so a simple
first leaf reads as normal rather than as being sold a lesser plant.
